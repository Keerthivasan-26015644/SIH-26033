## Problem Title
SIH 26033: Multiple intermediaries reduce farmers earnings and increase consumer prices.

## Problem Creater's Organization
Ministry of Consumer Affairs, Food & Public Distribution

## Theme
Agriculture, FoodTech & Rural Development

## Problem Description
Expected Solution: Create a digital marketplace that:

• Connects farmers/FPOs directly with consumers and bulk buyers.
• Provides logistics support.
• Uses AI for demand forecasting and route optimization.

Benefits:

• Better prices for farmers.
• Lower prices for consumers.
• Reduced supply chain inefficiencies.

## Proposed Solution
<b>1.1 Detailed explanation of the proposed solution!</b>

In the present agricultural supply chain, farmers often have to depend on different agents and wholesalers to sell their products. During this process, farmers may receive a lower price, while consumers end up paying more. Another problem is that small farmers usually find it difficult to contact large buyers because they cannot supply the required quantity individually.

To address these issues, we propose CropRelay, a platform that helps farmers sell their products directly to buyers with the support of digital technology.

The main idea behind CropRelay is to bring farmers, retailers, restaurants and consumers onto one platform. It also helps farmers combine their produce, compare prices and arrange transportation.

The platform will have the following features:

<b>1. Advance Order Booking</b>

Buyers such as restaurants, supermarkets and local shops can post their requirements before the harvesting period. They can mention the crop, required quantity, expected quality and delivery location.

Farmers can check these requirements and decide whether they can supply the products. This gives farmers a better idea of market demand before harvesting.

<b>2. Combining Produce from Small Farmers</b>

Many small farmers cannot fulfil large orders because they produce only limited quantities.

CropRelay solves this by allowing nearby farmers who grow the same crop to combine their produce for a single order.

For example, if five farmers have 100 kg of tomatoes each, the platform can combine their quantities to fulfil a buyer's requirement of 500 kg.

The quantity supplied by each farmer will be recorded separately so that payments can be calculated correctly.

<b>3. Price Comparison System</b>

Farmers should have the freedom to decide where and at what price they want to sell their products.

In CropRelay, different buyers can submit their price offers for available crops. Farmers can compare the offers along with the transportation and other service charges before accepting one.

This helps farmers make better selling decisions instead of depending on a single buyer.

<b>4. Shared Transportation</b>

Transportation costs can reduce the amount of money farmers actually earn.

CropRelay will identify nearby farmers and buyers who have compatible delivery requirements. It will suggest combining their deliveries into a common route.

For example, a vehicle can collect vegetables from multiple nearby farms and deliver them to different shops located along the same route.

This can reduce unnecessary travel and transportation expenses.

<b>5. QR-Based Crop Details</b>

Each group of produce will be given a unique QR code.

When a buyer scans the code, they can view details such as:

Name of the farmer or FPO.

Type of crop.

Quantity supplied.

Harvest date.

Quality details.

Collection and delivery status.

This helps buyers understand where their products came from and makes the supply process more transparent.

<b>1.2 How it addresses the problem</b>

In the existing system, agricultural products may pass through several agents before reaching consumers. Each stage can add extra charges and increase the final selling price.

CropRelay provides an alternative by connecting farmers and buyers directly and arranging the other necessary services through the platform.

<b>1.3 Innovation and uniqueness of the solution</b>

The main difference between CropRelay and an ordinary agricultural selling application is the way it handles demand, small quantities and transportation together.

<b>A. Demand Before Harvesting</b>

Buyers can share their requirements in advance. Farmers can use this information to plan their sales and reduce the uncertainty involved in finding buyers after harvesting.

<b>B. Small Quantity Combination</b>

The platform brings together produce from different small farmers to fulfil larger orders. Each farmer's contribution and payment will still be maintained separately.

<b>C. Delivery Based on Freshness</b>

Vegetables and fruits cannot be stored for the same amount of time. The system will consider the type of crop, harvesting time and delivery distance while suggesting transportation routes.

<b>D. Alternative Buyers for Unsold Produce</b>

If some produce remains unsold, the platform can notify nearby shops, food processing units and community kitchens. This gives farmers another opportunity to sell their products instead of allowing them to go to waste.

<b>E. Farmers Make the Final Decision</b>

The platform only suggests suitable buyers and available prices. The farmer has the final choice to accept or reject an offer.

<b>2.2 Methodology and process for implementation</b>

The working process of CropRelay will be as follows:

1. Farmers and buyers register on the platform.

2. Farmers enter the details of their available crops.

3. Buyers post their requirements, including quantity and delivery location.

4. The system compares the available crops with the buyer's requirements.

5. Nearby farmers with compatible products are grouped together.

6. Different buyers can submit their price offers.

7. Farmers compare the offers and select the one they prefer.

8. The system generates a QR code containing the crop details.

9. A suitable delivery route is suggested by considering nearby farms and buyers.

10. After delivery, the buyer confirms receipt and the payment records are updated.

2.3 Main modules of the prototype

The first version of the application will contain the following modules:

1. Farmer Dashboard: Farmers can register, add crop details and check available orders.

2. Buyer Dashboard: Buyers can post their requirements and view available products.

3. Crop Matching: Matches farmers' available products with buyer requirements.

4. Price Comparison: Displays different offers so farmers can choose a suitable price.

5. Delivery Tracking: Shows the collection and delivery status of products.

6. Payment History: Displays the quantity sold and payment details of each farmer.

These modules will help demonstrate the basic working of the proposed system.

## Feasibility and Viability
3.1 Analysis of the feasibility of the idea

CropRelay can be developed using technologies that are already available. It does not require expensive machines or special equipment to get started.

Technical Feasibility

The platform can be developed as a website and mobile application. Most of the required technologies are open-source or have free development options.

QR codes can be generated using software, and farmers can access them through ordinary smartphones. Mapping services can be used to plan delivery routes.

Economic Feasibility

The project can initially be tested in one village or a small agricultural area. Instead of starting across the entire country, we can begin with a few farmers and local buyers.

Farmer Producer Organisations can also help collect products and coordinate transactions.

A small service charge, if required, can be collected transparently to maintain the platform.

Operational Feasibility

Farmers do not need to completely change their existing selling methods. CropRelay can work alongside local markets and FPOs. Farmers who are not familiar with smartphones can receive assistance from a local collection centre.

3.2 Potential challenges and risks

Some difficulties may arise while implementing this project.

Some farmers may not be familiar with using mobile applications.

Internet connectivity may not be available in all agricultural areas.

Differences in crop quality may create problems between farmers and buyers.

Vehicles may not always be available at the required time.

Changes in weather and market demand may affect the quantity of available produce.

Fake buyers or sellers may create difficulties in transactions.

3.3 Strategies for overcoming these challenges

Provide a simple interface with regional language support, especially Tamil.

Allow users to save information and update it when internet connectivity becomes available.

Follow standard crop grading methods and provide an option for quality verification.

Maintain a list of available local transport providers.

Display demand estimates rather than guaranteeing that all produce will be sold.

Use mobile OTP verification and maintain proper transaction records.

## Impact and Benefits
4. Impact and Benefits

4.1 Potential impact on the target audience

The main users of CropRelay will be:

Small and marginal farmers.

Farmer Producer Organisations.

Vegetable and fruit retailers.

Restaurants and supermarkets.

Food processing companies.

Individual consumers.

Economic Benefits

Farmers get more opportunities to compare selling prices.

Unnecessary commission expenses can be reduced.

Shared transportation can lower delivery costs.

Buyers can find products directly from farming communities.

Transaction details become easier to understand.

Social Benefits

Small farmers get better access to digital markets.

Farmers can work together to fulfil larger orders.

Buyers can communicate directly with producers.

Farmers get more information about market requirements.

Environmental Benefits

Combining deliveries can reduce unnecessary vehicle trips.

Better planning can help reduce food wastage.

Unsold produce can be redirected to alternative buyers.

Better use of transportation can reduce fuel consumption.
